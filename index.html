Method and disclosure

Each task starts from one human clip. Its steps are annotated from frames, then a rigid-body scene is designed in MuJoCo from Opentrons labware definitions and parametric tools, and a scripted episode (velocity IK on a UR5e, calibrated width commands for a Robotiq 2F-85) reproduces the clip's object motion and task events. The embodiment, fixtures and plunger actuation are adapted, and every simplification is listed under the task's "Adaptation". Liquid is never simulated and no volumetric claim is made.

Every check is computed from simulator state (privileged information: poses, contacts, interpenetration) and reported as-is, failures included. A task passes only when every check passes, including interpenetration under 1 mm at every control tick, no robot self-contact, joints inside their ranges and end-effector tracking under 10 mm. Cameras are framed automatically so every end effector stays in view for the whole episode.

The Genesis video replays the exact per-tick commands of the MuJoCo episode in a second simulator from the same exported scene and initial state. Constraint switches (tip seating, cap thread) are emulated with run-time welds and joint limits; the divergence table under each task shows how far the tracked bodies drift between simulators. Friction grasps transfer poorly without Genesis-specific contact tuning, and that is shown rather than hidden.
